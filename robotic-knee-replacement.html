What is Robotic Knee Replacement?

Digital Planning and Navigation Added to a Surgeon-Led Knee Replacement

Robotic-assisted knee replacement uses technology to support planning, alignment assessment and execution of the surgical plan. The robot does not independently perform the operation.

The surgeon examines your knee, diagnosis and imaging
A personalised replacement plan is prepared
Robotic navigation supports alignment and positioning
Dr. Aman Singh remains in control of surgical decisions
Technology supports the surgeon
Is Robotic Surgery Better?

Robotic vs Conventional Knee Replacement

Both are surgeon-led procedures. The difference is the additional planning and navigation support available with robotic assistance.

Conventional Knee Replacement

  • Established surgical instruments and alignment methods
  • Surgeon plans and performs the procedure
  • Appropriate for many patients

Robotic-Assisted Knee Replacement

  • Adds digital planning and intra-operative navigation
  • Can support precise execution of the planned bone preparation and implant position
  • Suitability depends on the patient and surgeon's assessment
Robotic assistance does not guarantee a perfect result, 100% accuracy or 100% success.
Who is a Candidate?

Evaluation Comes Before Technology

The decision for knee replacement is based on symptoms, joint damage, examination, imaging, medical fitness and response to appropriate non-surgical treatment.

Reasons to Seek an Evaluation

  • Persistent pain affecting walking, stairs or sleep
  • Marked stiffness or reduced movement
  • Advanced arthritis or structural joint damage
  • Symptoms remaining disabling despite appropriate non-surgical care

Suitability for Robotic Assistance

  • Depends on diagnosis and anatomy
  • Requires surgeon assessment and surgical planning
  • May be considered for total or selected partial knee replacement cases
  • Medical conditions and individual recovery needs are reviewed

Implant Selection

Which Knee Implant Will Be Used?

Implant selection should be a clinical decision, not a marketing slogan. The recommended implant depends on diagnosis, anatomy, bone quality, surgical plan and availability.

Ask During Consultation

  • Which implant system is recommended for me?
  • Why is that implant suitable for my knee?
  • Is the plan total or partial replacement?
  • What implant-related costs are included in my estimate?

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions About Robotic Knee Replacement

Does the robot perform the surgery by itself?

No. The robotic system supports planning and navigation. The surgeon remains in control of the operation.

Is robotic knee replacement better than conventional surgery?

It provides additional planning and navigation tools, but whether it is the right approach depends on the individual patient and surgeon's assessment.

Which implant brand is used?

The recommended implant depends on clinical and surgical factors. Ask the orthopaedic team for the current implant systems available at JAP Hospital and why a particular option is recommended for you.

How long will I stay in hospital?

The expected stay is individualised according to your health, procedure, mobility and recovery. Your team should explain this before admission.

When will I be able to walk?

Assisted mobilisation is planned by the surgeon and physiotherapy team. Exact timing varies and should be given as part of your individual recovery plan.

What happens if there is a complication?

Any concern is assessed by the treating team, with monitoring, investigations and escalation to appropriate medical or surgical support when clinically required.

How much does robotic knee replacement cost?

Cost depends on the planned procedure, implant, medical needs, room category and other treatment components. Request a personalised estimate after evaluation.
