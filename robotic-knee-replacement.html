Robotic-Assisted Knee Replacement

Technology-assisted planning and navigation designed to support precise execution of a surgeon-led knee replacement plan.

What It Is

Robotic Assistance Supports the Surgeon—It Does Not Replace the Surgeon

Robotic-assisted knee replacement combines surgical planning with intra-operative navigation. Suitability depends on diagnosis, anatomy, overall health and the surgeon’s assessment.

Who May Need Knee Replacement?

  • Persistent knee pain limiting walking or daily activities
  • Significant stiffness or loss of movement
  • Advanced arthritis or joint damage
  • Symptoms not adequately controlled with appropriate non-surgical care

What Robotic Assistance Can Support

  • Detailed surgical planning
  • Alignment and positioning guidance
  • Real-time navigation during surgery
  • Execution of the surgeon’s personalised plan
Robotic vs Conventional

Different Tools, Same Surgeon-Led Decision Making

Conventional Knee Replacement

Uses established surgical instruments and alignment techniques. It remains a standard surgeon-led approach for many patients.

Robotic-Assisted Knee Replacement

Adds digital planning and navigation support that can help the surgeon execute the plan with greater precision. It is not appropriate for every patient.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions About Robotic Knee Surgery

Does the robot perform the surgery by itself?

No. Robotic systems are tools used by the surgeon for planning and navigation. The surgeon remains in control of the operation.

Is robotic surgery automatically better for everyone?

No. The potential value depends on the individual patient, diagnosis, anatomy and the surgeon’s assessment.

Can robotic assistance guarantee perfect results?

No medical procedure can guarantee 100% accuracy or 100% success. Outcomes depend on many patient and treatment factors.

How long is the hospital stay?

Hospital stay varies with the patient’s health, procedure and recovery. Your team will give you an individual plan.

When does physiotherapy begin?

Rehabilitation planning is generally part of the treatment pathway, but the exact timing and exercises are individualised.
